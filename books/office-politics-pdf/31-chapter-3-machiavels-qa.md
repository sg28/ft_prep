**Chapter 3 | Machiavels| Q&A**
**1.Question**
**What are the defining attributes of a Machiavel according**
**to Oliver James?**
Answer:A Machiavel typically exhibits a range of
traits such as resistance to social influences, the
ability to hide personal convictions, and a tendency
to change positions during arguments. They are

<!-- Page 81 -->

highly convincing, resistant to admitting mistakes,
and suspicious of others' motives. They operate
under the assumption that relationships aren't
reciprocal, may exploit situations for advantage, and
adapt strategies to suit circumstances.
**2.Question**
**How do non-Machiavels differ from Machiavels in their**
**approach to relationships and decisions?**
Answer:Non-Machiavels are more vulnerable to others'
opinions and often wear their convictions on their sleeves,
making them susceptible to manipulation. They tend to
accept others' motives at face value, assume reciprocity in
relationships, and may become inflexible in their positions.

In decision-making, they may struggle with negotiating
effectively and can appear unreasonable due to their
emotional reactions.
**3.Question**
**Why does the author suggest a measure of**
**Machiavellianism is necessary for self-advancement?**

Answer:Oliver James emphasizes that having some
Machiavellian traits is crucial for self-defense and career
progression in environments that can be competitive or
exploitative. It allows individuals to navigate social
dynamics strategically and protect themselves from
manipulation by others.
**4.Question**
**Can Machiavels be benign, and what is a notable example**
**of a benign Machiavel?**
Answer:Yes, not all Machiavels are self-serving; some are
benign and use their skills for the benefit of others. Notable
examples include leaders like Nelson Mandela or Mahatma
Gandhi, who utilized their strategies for the greater good
rather than personal gain.

**8.Question**
**What does the term 'triadic' imply in the context of**
**Machiavels?**
Answer:The term 'triadic' refers to a particularly dangerous
type of Machiavel who is not only manipulative and strategic
but also self-serving to the point where they may enjoy
causing harm or distress to others. Their motivations often
stem from a desire for power rather than rational ends.
**9.Question**
**What is the relationship between Machiavels and the**
**concept of manipulation?**
Answer:Machiavels are often skilled manipulators, using
their keen understanding of social dynamics to influence or
control others without seeming overtly manipulative. They
may engage in subtle tactics to achieve their goals, all while
maintaining a façade of normalcy.
