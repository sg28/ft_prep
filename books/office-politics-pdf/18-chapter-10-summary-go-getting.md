**Chapter 10 Summary : Go-getting**
**Chapter 10: Go-getting**
**Overview of Go-getting Tactics**
A set of strategies, including assertiveness, self-promotion,
feedback-seeking, negotiations, networking, and
reputation-building, can be employed to achieve goals
without being manipulative or aggressive.
**Assertiveness**
- Involves calmly asserting one’s views and setting clear
expectations.
- An assertive individual uses reasoning and may indicate
potential consequences for non-compliance.
- Individuals can be assertive without being extroverted. For
instance, a quiet manager can effectively say 'no' when
necessary.
- Examples illustrate the importance of strategic assertiveness
when interacting with superiors, as it is crucial to understand

<!-- Page 49 -->

when to voice opposition.
**Self-Promotion**
- Essential for showcasing competence and achieving
recognition.
- Must be balanced; excessive self-promotion can lead to
negative perceptions.
- Cultural context plays a significant role: self-promotion
may be required in competitive environments like the U.S.
but approached cautiously in collectivist cultures.
- The right timing and audience for self-promotion are
critical to avoid appearing boastful.
**Feedback-Seeking**
- Crucial in modern workplaces with limited feedback
mechanisms.
- Motivations for seeking feedback can be instrumental
(performance improvement), ego-based (self-esteem), or
image-based (reputation protection).
- People with growth mindsets tend to seek more feedback
and can handle criticism better.
- Cultural differences affect feedback-seeking behavior, with

<!-- Page 50 -->

individualistic cultures favoring personal achievement and
collectivist ones prioritizing group performance.
**Pay and Promotion Negotiations**
- Successful negotiations hinge on a clear understanding of
one’s value and preparation well in advance of discussions.
- Recognition of the disconnect between formal systems and
actual practices is important for effective negotiations.
- Building a good rapport with superiors and creating a
perception of hard work is essential for outcomes in salary
discussions.
**Networking**
- Essential for career advancement, networking involves
cultivating relationships that may not have immediate
benefits.
- Coalition-building and establishing social circles within
organizations can provide professional advantages.
- Effective networking relies on personality traits such as
affability, but anyone can develop these skills.
- A wide network can lead to opportunities and information
that facilitate career growth.

<!-- Page 51 -->

**Reputation-Building**
- Professional reputation influences promotion prospects and
career opportunities.
- Reputation is shaped by peers’ experiences and their
discussions about you, necessitating attention to
interpersonal relationships.
- Positive reputations can result in increased trust, influence,
and perceived competency.
- Key factors for leaders include educational background,
networking ability, and personal style, impacting how
leadership is perceived.
**Conclusion**
Being a proactive go-getter in the workplace enhances career
prospects. Success does not always require overt ambition;
rather, it thrives on strategic action, awareness of
interpersonal dynamics, and effective use of go-getting
tactics.
