**Chapter 3 Summary : Machiavels**
**Category**
**Traits**
Traits of Machiavels
                - Resistant to social influences and hides personal convictions.
                - Changes arguments easily and is convincing in the truth.
                - Resistant to admitting mistakes and suspicious of others' motives.
                - Expects no reciprocity in relationships and withholds judgment on others’ motives.
                - Adaptable strategies and exploitative, but not excessively vicious.
                - Not influenced by appeals for compliance and prefers dynamic environments.
                - Often preferred as leaders and work partners by peers.

Traits of
Non-Machiavels
                - Vulnerable to social influences and displays convictions openly.
                - Sticks rigidly to beliefs and readily confesses mistakes.
                - Less convincing in truth-telling and takes others' motives at face value.
                - Assumes reciprocity and has unrealistic expectations of others’ actions.
                - Locked into a single course of action and may seem unreasonable in negotiations.
                - Reluctant to exploit and seeks stable environments, which are diminishing.

Benign vs. Triadic
Machiavels
                - Some Machiavels use their skills for the greater good (benign), while triadic
Machiavels are self-serving and manipulative.
                - Not all identified Machiavels are triadic; discernment is essential for personal
protection.

Self-Defense and
Advancement
                - Develop independent ideas and avoid swaying with others’ opinions.
                - Conceal true convictions and show flexibility.
                - Practice cautious honesty and avoid naivety regarding others’ motives.
                - Prepare for the imperfect nature of relationships and avoid rigid thinking.
                - Manage emotions in negotiations strategically.
                - Adapt to the unstable work environments of today.

Case Study: Terry
                - Exemplifies a triadic Machiavel, known for building a successful property empire.
                - Employs charm and strategic manipulation to form valuable alliances.
                - Engages in self-serving behaviors and exhibits narcissistic tendencies.
                - Displays a lack of genuine human connection, treating relationships as strategic moves.

Understanding Triadic
Machiavels
                - Engage in complex game-playing and may target rivals through manipulation.
                - Can provide insightful advice to enemies, often leading to their downfall.
                - Less skilled Machiavels may reveal their motives openly and can be easier to identify.
                - Highly skilled triadic Machiavels do not act out of malice but as part of their
compulsive nature.
                - Awareness of their tactics may help individuals navigate the challenges posed by these
colleagues.
