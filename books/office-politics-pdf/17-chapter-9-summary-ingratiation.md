**Chapter 9 Summary : Ingratiation**
**Chapter 9: Ingratiation**
Ingratiation is a strategy used to gain favor from others by
making them feel good, thereby increasing the chances of
receiving what you want. Successful ingratiation requires
understanding the target person's preferences and executing
sincere tactics. The chapter focuses on three key methods of
ingratiation: chameleonism, flattery, and favor-rendering.
**Chameleonism**
Chameleonism involves mirroring another person’s
mannerisms or speech to create rapport. This behavior, often
unconscious, is seen in individuals who adapt to social
surroundings to enhance their social image. Constant
chameleons are adept as they strategically mimic others to
impress and gain favors. A self-assessment test is suggested
to gauge one's level of chameleonism, with implications that
higher scores may correlate with greater professional success
but can lead to ethical dilemmas and dissatisfaction.

**Flattery**
Flattery is the act of providing strategic praise to manipulate
others into favorable actions. While effective, it can backfire
if the recipient detects inauthenticity. Genuine compliments
are encouraged over exaggerated flattery to maintain trust.

The chapter illustrates the dynamics of flattery through
various examples, demonstrating its role in enhancing
professional relationships.
**Favor-Rendering**
Doing favors for others, called favor-rendering, is another
ingratiation method. By helping colleagues without
obligation, one can cultivate positive relationships and
enhance their reputation. Real-life examples showcase how
favor-rendering can lead to career advancements and improve
workplace dynamics, illustrating its importance in a
professional context. However, this tactic should be
employed strategically and not out of desperation for
approval.
