# The Intelligent Investor - Topics Index

A searchable reference guide to key concepts, principles, and topics.

## Core Concepts

### Margin of Safety
- **Definition**: The principle of purchasing securities at prices significantly below intrinsic value
- **Chapters**: 20 (primary), 1, 14, 15
- **Key Quote**: "Confronted with a like challenge to distill the secret of sound investment into three words, we venture the motto, MARGIN OF SAFETY"
- **Applications**: Bond coverage ratios, stock valuation below book value, diversification

### Mr. Market
- **Definition**: Allegory of market as manic-depressive business partner offering daily buy/sell prices
- **Chapter**: 8 (primary)
- **Key Principle**: Use market's emotional swings as opportunities; don't let them dictate your decisions
- **Application**: Buy when Mr. Market is pessimistic, sell when overly optimistic

### Defensive vs. Enterprising Investor
- **Defensive Investor**: Chapters 4, 5, 14
  - Seeks safety and freedom from worry
  - Minimal time commitment
  - Focus on high-quality bonds and diversified stocks
  - Requirements: 7 criteria for stock selection (Ch 14)

- **Enterprising Investor**: Chapters 6, 7, 15
  - Willing to devote time and effort
  - Seeks above-average returns
  - Focus areas: Unpopular large companies, bargain issues, special situations
  - Not about speculation or market timing

## Investment Strategies

### Stock Selection Criteria (Defensive - Chapter 14)
1. Adequate size of enterprise
2. Sufficiently strong financial condition
3. Earnings stability (10 years of positive earnings)
4. Dividend record (20+ years of continuous dividends)
5. Earnings growth (1/3 increase in EPS over 10 years)
6. Moderate P/E ratio (not more than 15x average earnings of past 3 years)
7. Moderate price-to-assets ratio (not more than 1.5x book value)

### Stock Selection Criteria (Enterprising - Chapter 15)
1. Financial condition: Current assets ≥ 2× current liabilities, long-term debt < working capital
2. Earnings stability: No deficit in past 5 years
3. Dividend record: Some current dividend
4. Earnings growth: Last year's earnings > 5 years ago
5. Price: Less than 120% of net tangible assets

### Portfolio Allocation
- **Basic Rule**: 25-75% in stocks, remainder in bonds
- **Default**: 50/50 stocks and bonds
- **Adjustment**: Based on opportunities, not market forecasts
- **Rebalancing**: Adjust when allocation drifts more than 5-10% from target

## Security Analysis

### Bond Analysis (Chapter 11)
- **Coverage Requirements for Investment-Grade Bonds**:
  - Industrials: 5× earnings coverage (or 3.2× after tax)
  - Utilities: 4× earnings coverage
  - Railroads: 3× earnings coverage
- **Focus**: Safety of principal, not growth potential
- **Yield**: Accept modest returns for stability

### Earnings Analysis (Chapter 12)
- **Adjustments Needed**:
  - Special charges and credits
  - Depreciation policies
  - Inventory valuation methods
  - Pension obligations
  - Stock options
- **Dilution**: Calculate fully diluted EPS including options, warrants, convertibles
- **Quality**: Prefer conservative accounting methods

### Company Comparisons (Chapters 13, 17, 18)
- Compare similar companies in same industry
- Examine operating ratios, financial strength, management quality
- Historical case studies: Penn Central, AAA Enterprises, NVF, Ling-Temco-Vought

## Market Psychology

### Speculation vs. Investment (Chapter 1)
- **Investment**: Thorough analysis, safety of principal, adequate return
- **Speculation**: Inadequate analysis, pursuit of profit through price changes
- **Intelligent Speculation**: Small portion of capital, never mistake it for investing

### Market Fluctuations (Chapter 8)
- **Three Approaches**:
  1. Timing (forecasting) - doesn't work
  2. Pricing (buying cheap, selling dear) - can work with discipline
  3. Buy-and-hold quality stocks - works for defensive investors
- **Formula Plans**: Generally ineffective once widely adopted

### Growth Stocks (Chapter 7)
- **Problem**: Great prospects already fully priced
- **Evidence**: Growth funds underperformed 1961-1970
- **Graham's View**: Avoid paying excessive premiums for growth
- **Alternative**: Buy growth companies when temporarily unpopular

## Investment Funds (Chapter 9)

### Performance Reality
- Most funds underperform market after fees
- Past performance doesn't predict future results
- "Hot" funds cool down due to: size, expenses, manager changes

### Recommendations
- **Index Funds**: Best choice for most investors
- **Closed-End Funds**: Often trade at discount to NAV
- **Selection Criteria**:
  - Low expenses (under 1%)
  - Manager owns significant shares
  - Willing to close to new investors
  - Doesn't advertise heavily

## Advisers and Services (Chapter 10)

### Types of Advisers
1. Investment counselors (for significant assets)
2. Trust services (banks)
3. Brokerage firms (conflicted - earn commissions)
4. Financial services (Moody's, S&P)

### Warning Signs
- Promises of high returns (>10% annually)
- Market timing claims
- Complex strategies you don't understand
- Excessive trading/commissions

### CFA Certification
- Graham instrumental in creating (1963)
- Professional standards for analysts
- Look for CFAs when seeking advice

## Corporate Behavior (Chapter 19)

### Dividend Policy
- **Graham's View**: Companies should pay out earnings unless they can earn above-average returns
- **Stock Dividends**: Usually meaningless, cosmetic
- **Share Buybacks**: Can be intelligent if stock undervalued
- **Retention**: Only justified if company can profitably reinvest

### Management Quality Indicators
- Conservative accounting
- Reasonable executive compensation
- Shareholder-friendly policies
- Long-term thinking vs. quarterly focus

## Historical Examples

### Successful Bargain Investments
- **A&P (1938)**: Stock at $36 with $85M cash, recovered to 117½
- **Chrysler (1962)**: Classic turnaround, multiple opportunities to buy cheap

### Cautionary Tales (Chapter 17)
- **Penn Central**: Railroad bankruptcy (1970) - inadequate earnings, financial manipulation
- **Ling-Temco-Vought**: Conglomerate collapse - excessive leverage, acquisition spree
- **NVF**: Hostile takeover disaster - destroyed both companies
- **AAA Enterprises**: Franchise boom and bust - unsustainable business model

### Zweig's Modern Examples
- **Inktomi**: $25B market cap → $40M (Mr. Market's mania)
- **Dot-com Bubble**: 1999-2002 crash validated Graham's principles
- **Growth Stock Warnings**: Sun Microsystems, JDS Uniphase crashes

## Special Topics

### Convertible Bonds and Warrants (Chapter 16)
- **Convertibles**: Hybrid securities, usually overpriced
- **Warrants**: Long-term call options, highly speculative
- **Graham's View**: Avoid unless trading at obvious bargain prices

### Inflation Protection (Chapter 2)
- **Stocks**: Some inflation protection through business growth
- **Bonds**: No inflation protection, but provide stability
- **TIPS**: Modern solution (Zweig's addition)
- **Real Estate**: Can hedge inflation but requires expertise

### Tax Considerations
- Preferential treatment for long-term capital gains
- Tax-loss harvesting to offset gains
- Municipal bonds for high tax brackets
- IRA/401(k) for tax-deferred growth

## Practical Applications

### Getting Started Checklist
- [ ] Determine your investor personality (defensive or enterprising)
- [ ] Establish stock/bond allocation (default 50/50)
- [ ] For defensive investors: Apply 7 stock selection criteria
- [ ] For enterprising investors: Look for bargains, unpopular large caps
- [ ] Never invest on margin or borrow to invest
- [ ] Ignore market predictions and hot tips
- [ ] Review portfolio quarterly, rebalance annually
- [ ] Focus on long-term (3-5 years minimum)

### Red Flags to Avoid
- Stocks trading at >15× average 3-year earnings (defensive)
- Stocks trading at >1.5× book value (defensive)
- Companies with negative earnings in past 5 years
- High debt loads (debt > working capital)
- No dividend history for "safe" stocks
- Excessive management compensation
- Accounting irregularities or restatements
- Rapid acquisition sprees
- New issues during market euphoria

### Research Resources
- **Then (Graham's Era)**: Moody's, S&P manuals, annual reports
- **Now (Zweig's Additions)**: SEC EDGAR, company websites, Morningstar, financial news
- **Focus**: Facts and figures, not opinions and forecasts

## Conclusion

Graham's core message: "To achieve satisfactory investment results is easier than most people realize; to achieve superior results is harder than it looks."

The book provides a complete framework for:
1. Protecting yourself from serious mistakes
2. Establishing investment policies
3. Selecting securities intelligently
4. Managing emotions and psychology
5. Maintaining discipline through market cycles

Success requires:
- Patience
- Discipline
- Willingness to go against the crowd
- Focus on facts, not forecasts
- Margin of safety in every decision
