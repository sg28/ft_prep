# STAR Story 5 — Failure / Learning (DRAFT TEMPLATE)

> ⚠️ **This story is invented, not a real account of your work.** The original plan marks this "TO DRAFT" with no content — nothing in the plan describes an actual failure from your career, so there's no real incident to draw from. What follows is a template built from the plan's own criteria, showing the shape a strong answer should take. **Replace it with a real failure before using it in an interview** — a fabricated story falls apart under the follow-up matrix (interviewers will ask "what did your manager think?", "walk me through your first week", etc., and an invented story has no real answer for those). Using this as-is would be dishonest and is likely to be caught.

## Criteria (from the plan)

- You owned it
- Real consequences
- Behavior changed after — a specific, concrete change, not vague "I learned to communicate better"

## Template Shape (pick the pattern closest to something real from your career)

**Pattern:** Shipped a bug that caused a customer-facing incident.

> **Situation:** [Specific: what shipped, what broke, who was affected, how it was discovered — e.g. "I shipped a change to X that passed review and tests but caused Y for a subset of users in production."]
>
> **Task:** [Your specific responsibility in the incident — were you the author, the reviewer, the on-call responder?]
>
> **Action — including the wrong call:** [What you actually did, and be explicit about the mistake itself, not just the fix. E.g., "I assumed the existing test coverage caught this class of edge case — it didn't, because [specific reason]." The wrong call has to be a real decision, not "I was unlucky."]
>
> **Result — the actual damage, owned squarely:** [A real number or real consequence — customers affected, revenue impact, SLO breach, trust damage with a stakeholder. Naming the actual size of the damage is what makes this credible; softening it reads as evasive.]
>
> **Learning — the specific behavioral change since:** [Not "I learned to test more" — something concrete and checkable, e.g. "Since then I require an edge-case checklist reviewed by a second engineer before merging anything touching X" or "I added a specific type of test/monitoring that would have caught this class of bug, and it has caught N similar issues since."]

## Patterns to Avoid (from the plan)

- Failures that aren't really failures ("I worked too hard and burned out")
- Blaming the team or manager
- Failures with no learning attached

## Follow-Ups to Prep For (once you have a real story here)

- What was your manager's reaction?
- What would you have done differently in the first hour after discovering it?
- Has the specific change you made actually prevented a recurrence, or is that unproven?
