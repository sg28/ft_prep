# Behavioral Answer — Strengths and Weaknesses

## Strength

**Cross-functional translation** — with evidence:

> "I'm comfortable bridging product, design, security, and platform engineering. Last year I led a developer portal program across 8+ teams, and the hardest part wasn't the engineering — it was aligning what 'golden path' meant to each team. I brought them to consensus and shipped."

This ties directly to [Story 3 — Cross-functional Leadership](../star-stories/story-3-cross-functional-leadership.md) if the interviewer asks for more detail — have that story ready to expand into.

## Weakness

**Defaults to building rather than evaluating** — real, with a stated mitigation:

> "I default to building rather than evaluating. When I see a problem I tend to start prototyping before fully scoping alternatives. I've worked on this by forcing myself to write a one-page tech spec listing options + tradeoffs before any code. The Strapi CMS evaluation is an example — I made myself evaluate 4 options before committing, which I wouldn't have done two years ago."

This ties to [Story 2 — Cost Discipline](../star-stories/story-2-cost-discipline.md) — the Strapi migration is concrete proof the stated mitigation actually happened, not just a claimed intention.

## What to Avoid

Don't use:
- "I work too hard."
- "I'm a perfectionist."
- "I care too much."

These read as evasive non-answers — they signal you're avoiding a real weakness rather than showing self-awareness. A real weakness with a concrete, evidenced mitigation is a stronger answer than a fake weakness with no cost.
