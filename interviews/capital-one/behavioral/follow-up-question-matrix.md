# Behavioral Answer — Follow-Up Question Matrix

These generic follow-ups apply to *any* STAR story — interviewers use them to dig past the rehearsed headline. Story-specific answers to these (where the plan already has them) live inside each file in [../star-stories/](../star-stories/). This file is the general approach to each type of follow-up, for stories that don't have one pre-written.

| Follow-up | What they're testing | How to answer it |
|---|---|---|
| What was your *specific* contribution vs the team's? | Are you taking credit for group work? | Name your exact deliverable — the piece only you did — separately from what the team did around you. Use "I" for your part, "we" for the team's. |
| What would you do differently? | Self-awareness, learning | Always have a real answer ready — never "nothing, it went great." Pick one concrete decision you'd revisit, not a vague platitude. |
| How did you measure success? | Outcomes orientation | A number, always. If the story doesn't have one memorized, that's a gap to fix before the interview, not during it. |
| What was the hardest part technically? | Depth | Go one level deeper than the headline — the specific tradeoff, edge case, or failure mode you had to solve, not just "it was complex." |
| How did stakeholders react? Anyone push back? | Influence, conflict handling | Name a real point of friction and how you resolved it — a story with zero pushback reads as either untrue or not actually hard. |
| What did your manager think? | Self-perception calibration | Answer honestly, including if feedback was mixed — a suspiciously perfect answer here reads as unreflective. |
| If you had half the time, what would you cut? | Prioritization | Name the actual lowest-value piece of scope, and why it was safe to cut — shows you understood the priority order, not just the task list. |
| Walk me through your first week on this | Concreteness check | This one catches fabricated or exaggerated stories — if you can't describe concrete early steps, the story wasn't real ownership. |

## General Principle

The follow-up matrix exists because the headline STAR answer is rarely what gets evaluated — the follow-ups are. Prep each of the 6 STAR stories against every row in this table, not just the ones the plan already answered, since any interviewer can pick a different follow-up than the ones anticipated.
